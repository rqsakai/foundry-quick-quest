# QuickQuest — The Player's Book

> Source of truth for every rule the system encodes. Converted from the owner's
> `quickquest-players-book.html` on 2026-10-19. Change the rule here first, then the code.

*Four numbers, one die, and no wrong answers. Everything you need to be a hero fits on one
sheet of paper.*

## How the Game Works

*One die. Low stakes. Big stories.*

You have four numbers, and they describe your whole hero. **STR** is strength: hitting hard,
lifting, breaking. **AGI** is agility: speed, aim, sneaking. **CON** is constitution: how much
you can take. **INT** is intelligence: magic and knowing things. Spread **10 points** across them
— minimum 1, maximum 5. A balanced hero is 3/3/2/2; a specialist is 5/3/1/1. Both work.

**The only math in the game:** Life = 7 + CON × 2 · Magic Points = INT × 3

### The Roll

When something is risky and failure would be interesting, roll a d20. Meet or beat the target
number and you succeed. You do not add your stats — a mighty hero and a scrawny goblin swing
the same die. What makes you better is your gear, your spells, and your ideas.

Except when you're truly great at something. If the stat that fits the task is **4 or 5**, roll
the check with **advantage** — 2d20, take the better. STR 5 kicks the door with advantage; INT 5
remembers the old legend with advantage. **Checks only, never attack rolls.** And advantage never
stacks: there is no 3d20.

### Twenties and Ones

**Natural 20:** critical hit — automatic double damage. **Natural 1:** total failure, and
something goes wrong. The Master rolls a d6:

| d6 | What happens |
|---|---|
| 1 | Your weapon flies out of your hand, ten feet away |
| 2 | You trip and fall over |
| 3 | You hit the nearest wall, floor, or piece of furniture instead |
| 4 | You're wide open — the next attack against you hits automatically |
| 5 | Something loud breaks, and everyone nearby hears it |
| 6 | You do it perfectly. To the wrong target. |

Fumbles are funny, never devastating. Nobody dies from a natural 1.

### Distance

Three distances, no measuring. **TOUCHING** — sword reach. **NEARBY** — same room. **FAR** —
you can see it, you can't reach it this turn. Your move takes you one step along that ladder.
Melee needs touching; bows and slings reach far; each spell says how far it goes.

## Fighting

*Players first. Then everything with teeth.*

### Turn Order

Players always go first — in whatever order they shout out. Then all the monsters. The one
exception: if the story says you were surprised (you walked into the ambush, you opened the
chest that was a mouth), the monsters go first that round.

### Your Turn

Move + one action. Walk somewhere and do one thing: attack, cast a spell, drink a potion, shove
the statue, grab the lever. Talking is free — shout all you want.

**Opportunity attack:** run past or through an enemy close enough to touch you, and they get
one free swing as you go by. Backing away is safe. Sprinting through the middle of three orcs is
not.

### The Attack, in Three Steps

| Step | What you do |
|---|---|
| 1 | Roll d20 vs their Defense. Meet or beat it and you hit. No modifiers — unless a spell, hex, or magic item says so. |
| 2 | Roll damage: your weapon's die + STR (heavy weapons) or AGI (light and ranged ones). |
| 3 | Subtract from their Life. At 0 they're down and out of the fight. |

**Advantage & Disadvantage.** Great position — behind them, above them, they're asleep? Roll
2d20, take the better. Terrible position — in the dark, hanging off a ledge? Take the worse.
This is the only bonus in the game, and it needs no math.

### Going Down, and Death

Nobody dies from reaching 0 Life. You're down — unconscious, out of the fight — until someone
treats you, heals you, or the danger passes.

If the whole party goes down, the story gets worse; it doesn't end. You wake up captured.
Robbed. Washed up downstream. Failure is a plot twist, never a game over.

There is exactly one way to die: choosing it. Any player, at any moment, may declare a
**sacrifice** — hold the bridge, shield a friend, carry the bomb out. A sacrifice always works:
whatever you died for, you get. You narrate your own ending, and the table remembers it forever.

## Make Your Hero

*Five minutes, seven steps, one legend.*

1. Spread 10 points across STR · AGI · CON · INT (1–5 each)
2. Life = 7 + CON × 2 · Magic Points = INT × 3
3. Pick a race — take its trait
4. Pick a profession — take its move, its Ace, and its gear
5. Pick one cantrip — yes, even the barbarian
6. Note your Defense (from armor) and your weapon's damage die
7. Name. Done.

### Races

| Race | Trait |
|---|---|
| Human | **Lucky.** Once per session, reroll any die. |
| Elf | **Night vision.** You see in the dark as if it were dim light. |
| Dwarf | **Stone sense.** You know when rock is unstable, hollow, or hiding a door. |
| Halfling | **Small and quick.** Hide behind almost anything; squeeze through gaps others can't. |
| Orc | **Fierce.** Once per fight, add +2 to one damage roll. |
| Gnome | **Small-speech.** Talk with animals no bigger than a dog. |

### Professions

| Profession | Move | Starting gear |
|---|---|---|
| Warrior | **Guard.** When an attack would hit someone next to you, take it instead. | Sword, shield, chainmail, 20 gold. |
| Barbarian | **Rage.** Once per fight: +2 damage, −2 Defense for the rest of it. | Greataxe, leather armor, 10 gold. |
| Rogue | **Sneak Attack.** Attacking someone who hasn't noticed you: 2d20 take the better, +2 damage. | Two daggers, bow, leather, lockpicks, rope, 15 gold. |
| Wizard | Magic Points = INT × 3. Knows two spells. | Staff, clothes, spellbook, 10 gold. |
| Healer | Magic Points = INT × 3. Knows Healing Hands + one more. | Mace, leather, bandages, 15 gold. |

Warriors, barbarians and rogues have 0 Magic Points. They don't need them.

**Mid-fight hiding.** Want to be unseen again? Spend your turn hiding — a check vs 12 with
something to hide behind. Succeed, and your next attack counts as a Sneak Attack.

## Ready-Made Heroes

*Steal one whole, or bend one until it's yours.*

A specialist puts a 5 in one stat: blinding at one thing, advantage on its checks, and praying
about everything else. A generalist spreads out: never amazing, never helpless, always holding
an answer. Neither is better — a good party has both. Every hero below is a legal 10-point
build with the math done. Swap the race freely; the trait comes with it.

## Your Ace

*Once per session. Only when your back is against the wall.*

Every hero holds one Ace — a single enormous thing they can do, once per session, never twice.
You can't play it whenever you like: each Ace only works when things are already bad. That's the
point. An Ace is not a spell you cast; it's the moment the music changes.

| Profession | Ace | Plays when | Effect |
|---|---|---|---|
| Warrior | **Unbreakable** | you drop to 0 Life. | You don't fall. You stay standing at 1 Life, and nothing can take you below 1 until the fight is over. When it ends, so do you — you collapse and need treating like anyone else. |
| Barbarian | **Blood Frenzy** | you're at half Life or less. | For the rest of the fight, every attack of yours that lands rolls damage twice and takes the higher. You also take double damage from everything. There is no calling it off. |
| Rogue | **The Opening** | you can see the enemy and they haven't seen you move. | You've been waiting the whole fight. Your attack hits automatically and counts as a critical — double damage, plus your Sneak Attack bonus. No roll. You just say where the dagger goes. |
| Wizard | **Final Word** | your Magic Points are at 0. | Empty, exhausted, and still standing. Cast any spell you know, for free, at double effect — double damage, double healing, double duration. The most dangerous wizard in the room is the one with nothing left to lose. |
| Healer | **Second Breath** | at least one ally is at 0 Life. | Every ally you can see gets back on their feet and recovers half their missing Life. Costs no Magic Points. It costs you only the fact that you'll never do it again today. |

**A second Ace?** Very rarely, a treasure hoard holds something that lets a hero play their Ace
a second time. If you ever find one, guard it like a crown.

## Magic

*Cantrips are endless. Spells are for the moment that matters.*

Casting is automatic — spend the points, it works. Twelve spells exist in the whole world.
Wizards know two; healers know Healing Hands plus one. Magic Points come back only three ways:
mana crystals (treasure), places of power (rest there and refill completely), and **channeling**
— casting with your own body, at 2 Life per missing point, once per fight, no more.

| Spell | Cost | Range | Effect |
|---|---|---|---|
| Fireball | 3 | Far | d6 damage to everything in a small area. Everything. Check where your friends are. |
| Summon Lightning | 3 | Far | d8 to one target you can see. Ignores their Defense entirely — it just hits. |
| Healing Hands | 2 | Touching | Restore d6+2 Life. The only thing that gets someone up from 0 mid-fight. |
| Shield of Air | 2 | Nearby | You or an ally gets +3 Defense until the fight ends. |
| Sleep | 3 | Nearby | One creature sleeps until shaken or hurt. Not the very large; not the undead. |
| Bless | 1 | Nearby | An ally rolls 2d20, takes the better, on their next attack. |
| Grasping Vines | 2 | Nearby | Vines hold one creature. It fights but can't move; escaping takes its turn + STR vs 12. |
| Frost Bite | 2 | Nearby | d6 damage, and frozen feet — no move on its next turn. |
| Double | 2 | Self | A shimmering twin. The next attack that would hit you hits it, and it pops. |
| Reveal | 1 | Nearby | Hidden things glow: secret doors, tracks, invisible creatures, poison in the cup. |
| Purify | 1 | Touching | Cleanse poison or disease from food, drink, or a person. |
| Whispering Wind | 1 | Far | One sentence, into the ear of anyone you can see. Only they hear it. |

### Cantrips — pick one, keep it forever

| Cantrip | Effect |
|---|---|
| Spark | Light a candle, fuse, or campfire from a few feet away. |
| Mend | Fix one broken small object. |
| Light | An object glows like a torch for an hour. |
| Ghost Sound | A noise of your choice, from where you point. |
| Chill | Cool a drink, freeze a puddle, frost a doorknob. |
| Float | Lift something book-sized across the room. |
| Clean | One object or person becomes spotless. |
| Trickster | A tiny illusion, hand-sized. It can't touch anything. |

Cantrips are free, endless, and never solve a fight. They exist to be clever with. Be as weird
as possible.

### Getting Life back

**Treating wounds:** bandages + ten quiet minutes = half your missing Life back, rounded up,
once per fight. **Healing Hands and potions:** d6+2, instantly. Drinking a potion is your whole
turn.
